# CISO Positioning & Authority Model

## Reporting line

The CISO reports to the **CIO** for day-to-day alignment, with a **direct, documented escalation channel to the CEO and the board** (quarterly board slot, ad hoc for critical incidents). This preserves independence when security and IT delivery objectives conflict.

## Authority

- **Veto on go-live** of changes that create a critical risk without an accepted treatment (used once: DEC-002).
- **Ownership of incident command** and of regulatory notification preparation (NIS2 Art. 23, GDPR Art. 33).
- **Definition of minimum security requirements** (policies, baselines, supplier clauses).
- **Formal opinion** on every risk acceptance above the "High" threshold; the decision itself belongs to the risk owner.

## Limits (by design)

- No ownership of IT or product budgets; the CISO proposes and justifies (see ROSI in the board pack).
- No operational management of engineering teams.
- The CISO does not *accept* risk: business owners do, in writing, for a limited period.

## Structural tension

Security introduces friction by design. The role is to make that friction **proportionate, explained and arbitrated at the right level**, not to remove it — see the decision journal for concrete trade-offs.
